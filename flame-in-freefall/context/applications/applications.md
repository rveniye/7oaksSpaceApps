# Finding fire-safe materials
NASA's SoFIE experiments tested the flammability of plexiglass, cotton-based fabrics, and other materials commonly used in spaceflight. Although SoFIE's purpose was to study the safety of spacecraft fires, the data collected could improve fire safety on Earth by improving screening tests to evaluate fire-safe materials for homes, offices, aircraft, and other uses.

# Reducing harmful combustion products
One ACME investigation called Flame Design aimed to study how much soot was produced by flames under different flame conditions. Soot is the solid carbon produced by flames when incomplete combustion occurs. It can cause environmental and health issues: it warms the atmosphere, contributing to global warming, and inhalation can lead to respiratory diseases and cardiovascular issues. However, there are situations where soot is desirable, such as in some culinary or industrial applications. The results of the investigation could aid in the design of flames that produce more or less soot and may help create more efficient and less polluting flames.

FLEX led to the discovery of a type of flame called a cool flame, which continues to burn after the extinction of the visible flame in certain conditions. Different to regular flames, cool flames typically burn at about 400 °C and instead of producing carbon dioxide and water, cool flames produce carbon monoxide and formaldehyde. Cool flame research could lead to cleaner auto ignition through the use of HCCI (Homogeneous Charge Compression Ignition) engines. HCCI technology creates a less polluting combustion process, because it requires lower combustion temperatures, reducing the production of nitrogen oxides, and leads to more efficient combustion, using less fuel per mile.

# Improvements to firefighting
Project FIRES (Firefighter's Integrated Response Equipment System) aimed to apply advanced materials and design concepts from Apollo to improve firefighting gear that did not adequately protect from the dangers of fire suppression activity. Lightweight, fire-resistant, heat-protective materials originally developed for use in space suits and spacecraft can be used to create safer firefighting gear. Additionally, Scott Aviation and NASA's Johnson Space Center collaborated to develop lightweight firefighter's air tanks, only 20 pounds for a 30-minute supply.
