# Fine water mist
The ISS currently uses this. Roughly 6 pounds of ionised water is sealed within a flexible titanium metal bellows. Outside the bellows is 1.2 pounds of nitrogen gas stored at high pressure. When an astronaut squeezes the handle, the water is forced towards the nozzle regardless of the orientation of the extinguisher due to 0g. Just before exiting, the water and high-pressure nitrogen mix in a specialised Venturi nozzle. The now expanding nitrogen gas force the water apart into a super-fine mist of droplets of 10-50 micrometres wide. Because the water droplets are microscopic and spherical, their total surface area to volume ratios are high. The mist evaporates immediately into steam and absorbs massive amounts of thermal energy, dropping the temperature below combustion levels. Additionally, when the water particles evaporate, they expand in volume by up to 1,700 times. This cloud of team temporarily pushes oxygen away from the flame, starving it. This works both on and off spacecrafts.
