# Ventilation and flamelet extinction

Airflow supplies oxygen and carries heat away. In NASA Glenn's confined-channel experiments, increasing airflow caused surviving flamelets to flare up and merge into a continuous flame front.

The demonstrated cooling sequence in Saffire V was **reduce flow → allow cooling → stop flow**. For PMMA samples V-3 and V-4, flow was reduced from 20 to 5 cm/s for 300 seconds. The samples extinguished immediately when the fan was then stopped. In the separate cotton test V-2, fire grew back when flow resumed after 70 seconds without forced flow. Surviving flamelets and embers were seen before imaging stopped; which sustained combustion throughout the remaining interval was not determined.

Briefly opening ventilation and then shutting it off is a different proposal. These sources do not establish it as a reliable flamelet-extinction method. These experimental results describe specific fuels and conditions, not a cabin operating procedure. Ventilation here means forced internal airflow, not opening the cabin to vacuum.
