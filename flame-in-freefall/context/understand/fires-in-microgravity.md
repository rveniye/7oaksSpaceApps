# How are fires different in microgravity?
Shape – On earth, buoyancy drives hot air upwards, leading to the formation of the usual teardrop shape. However, in space where gravity, and therefore buoyancy, is not present, the fire burns equally in all directions, forming a sphere in perfect conditions. 
Temperature - Due to the lack of convention currents for the same reason, scientists can create ‘cool flames’ which burn at much lower temperature than normal fire.
How are they caused?
1.	Electronic malfunction – short circuiting, overheating wires, and faulty batteries /other electrical components, can all lead to fires.
2.	Overheating equipment 
3.	Flammable materials – due to the oxygen rich environment, combustion becomes more intense and easier. Example – In the Mir space station in 1997, a canister of lithium perchlorate (the chemical burned to produce oxygen in the station) burst into flame.
